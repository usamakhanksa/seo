What Changed by 2026

A Moving Target, Not a Frozen Checklist

Google's public ranking history shows continued major changes through 2025 and 2026. Here's what matters for your prompt strategy.

🔄

Core & Spam Updates

March 2026 core and spam updates, May 2026 core update, June 2026 spam update, plus earlier 2025 core and spam updates. The ranking landscape keeps evolving—your prompts must evolve too.

🎯

Helpful Content Is Baseline

Google's "helpful content" system became part of the core ranking systems in March 2024. "Optimize for helpful content" is no longer a niche tactic—it is the baseline for all modern SEO work.

🤖

AI Search: Same Fundamentals

Google's guidance confirms the same foundational SEO best practices apply to AI Overviews and AI Mode. No extra technical requirements, no special schema, no "AI file" needed.

❌

Outdated Tactics Removed

Google removed FAQ rich results in 2026. LLMs.txt files are confirmed as not affecting rankings. Stop treating these as shortcuts—they're dead ends.

📱

Technical Fundamentals

Mobile-first indexing, JavaScript crawl-render-index phases, Core Web Vitals (INP under 200ms), robots.txt for crawl management only, and sitemap submission as hints only.

📊

Measurement Matters

AI feature traffic is included in Search Console under Web search type. Bing's IndexNow is central for freshness. Your prompts must include measurement and post-publication tracking.

Audit of the Original Prompt Pack

The original pack covers the right buckets—but many prompts are too generic for 2026 and reflect older SEO habits.

🔍 What's Right

The original pack covers keyword research, on-page SEO, technical SEO, and content writing. That's a solid starting structure. The broad categories are sound.

⚠️ What Needs Correction

Many prompts lean heavily on generic ideation, rigid title/meta "limits," and light technical scopes. Modern documentation puts more emphasis on helpful people-first content, search appearance accuracy, mobile parity, crawlable internal links, canonicalization, JavaScript rendering, structured data validation, and post-publication measurement.

  • 1
    Title tags & meta descriptions should not be treated like fixed-character formulas. Google says titles should be descriptive and concise, but they're truncated as needed. Meta descriptions have no hard length limit; snippets are primarily generated from page content.
  • 2
    "Optimized URLs" should not imply slugs alone communicate hierarchy. Google uses links and site relationships to infer importance. Readable URLs help, but internal linking and crawlable navigation matter more.
  • 3
    Technical prompts covering only sitemaps and robots.txt are incomplete in 2026. Canonicalization, mobile-first indexing, JavaScript rendering, structured data, and Core Web Vitals are all part of the default workflow.

    KR-01

    Build a Business-Aligned Topic Universe

    ▼
    Act as a senior SEO strategist for [brand/site] in [country/language]. Based on these inputs — products/services: [list], audience segments: [list], conversion goals: [list], and known restrictions: [list] — generate 40 topic clusters instead of simple keywords. For each cluster, include: search intent, core entity, likely funnel stage, suggested pillar page, 5 supporting article ideas, and why the cluster matters commercially. Exclude topics that are off-brand, low-trust, or only loosely related.
    Why it's better: It forces the model to think in clusters, entities, and business value, not just keyword volume.
    KR-02

    Map Intent and SERP Expectations

    ▼
    For these target queries — [paste list] — classify each one into primary intent: informational, commercial investigation, transactional, navigational, or local. Then predict the most likely SERP expectations for each query, such as comparison content, tutorial, category page, product page, video, image-heavy content, or local results. Tell me which page type I should create or improve.
    Why it's better: 2026 SEO is not just about keywords; it's about matching the result type users and search systems expect.
    KR-03

    Run a Realistic Content Gap Analysis

    ▼
    Compare [my site] against [competitor 1], [competitor 2], and [competitor 3]. Identify topic clusters they cover well that my site either does not cover or covers weakly. Separate the findings into: missing topics, weak topics, outdated topics, and topics I should ignore because they are not relevant to my brand or audience. Prioritize by business relevance first, then likely search demand, then competitive difficulty.
    Why it's better: It avoids the bad habit of copying every competitor topic without relevance filtering.
    KR-04

    Pull Winning Opportunities from Search Console

    ▼
    Using this Google Search Console export for the last [X] months, identify pages and queries with the highest upside. Focus on: pages ranking roughly positions [5–20] with good impressions, pages with below-expected CTR, and queries where multiple pages may be competing. Return a prioritized table with: page, query, issue type, likely fix, expected impact, and whether the page should be refreshed, consolidated, or left alone.
    Why it's better: It turns keyword research into performance-driven optimization.
    KR-05

    Add Trend and Seasonality Intelligence

    ▼
    For these topics or products — [list] — create a seasonal opportunity map for [country]. Tell me which topics are evergreen, which are seasonal, which are rising, and which are declining. Then recommend a 12-month publishing calendar with publish windows, refresh windows, and promotional windows. If exact trend data is unavailable, state what should be verified in Google Trends.
    Why it's better: It reflects Google's guidance to use Trends for rising and seasonal demand, not just static keyword lists.
    KR-06

    Localize Keyword Opportunities Properly

    ▼
    Take these English keyword clusters — [list] — and adapt them for [target market/language]. Do not translate literally. Instead, suggest how real search behavior may differ by country, spelling, measurement system, pricing language, local regulations, or cultural terms. Separate exact translations from true local search variants, and explain which version should be the canonical content angle.
    Why it's better: It aligns keyword work with multilingual and local-market realities rather than word-for-word translation.
    KR-07

    Detect Cannibalization and Consolidation Gaps

    ▼
    Using this list of URLs, target keywords, and Search Console queries — [paste/export] — identify likely cannibalization problems. Show where multiple pages appear to target the same intent, where one new page should replace several thin pages, and where internal links or canonicals may be enough without merging content. Recommend the safest consolidation plan.
    Why it's better: It moves keyword research closer to site architecture and index quality.
    OP-01

    Generate a Search-Friendly URL Recommendation

    ▼
    I am creating a page about [topic] for [audience/country]. Suggest 5 SEO-friendly URL slug options that are readable, durable, and aligned with the site structure [paste structure if known]. Avoid unnecessary dates, fragile parameters, and vague words. Then recommend the best final slug and explain how it should fit into the wider site architecture.
    Why it's better: It keeps the slug readable without pretending the slug alone solves architecture.
    OP-02

    Rewrite Title Links for Click Clarity

    ▼
    For this page — topic: [topic], intent: [intent], audience: [audience], current title: [title] — write 10 improved title options. Make them descriptive, concise, non-spammy, and clearly differentiated. Include 3 safer versions, 3 benefit-led versions, 2 comparison-style versions, and 2 brand-led versions. Then explain which one is best and why Google might prefer or rewrite weak alternatives.
    Why it's better: It reflects Google's emphasis on clarity and anti-keyword-stuffing, not arbitrary formulas.
    OP-03

    Write Meta Descriptions That Match the Page

    ▼
    Using the page summary below — [paste page summary] — create 6 meta description options that accurately summarize the page, highlight the most decision-useful details, and avoid vague filler or keyword stuffing. For each option, tell me which searcher intent it best fits. If the page content is too weak to support a strong snippet, say so and tell me what the page is missing.
    Why it's better: It respects the fact that Google often uses page content for snippets and only uses the meta description when it helps.
    OP-04

    Build a Heading Architecture Before Writing

    ▼
    Create an H1–H3 outline for a page targeting [primary query] and [secondary topics]. The outline should reflect the user journey from basic understanding to decision-making. Avoid repetitive near-duplicate headings. Mark which section is best for a quick answer block, which sections deserve examples, and which sections should include supporting media.
    Why it's better: It turns headings into information architecture rather than just keyword placements.
    OP-05

    Optimize for Answer Blocks and AI-Supporting Passages

    ▼
    Take this draft or outline — [paste] — and identify the sections most likely to support featured snippets, AI Overviews, or other answer-style retrieval. Rewrite only those sections into clearer, self-contained answer blocks that directly address the question, use precise language, and stay faithful to the page's larger argument. Do not invent facts. Show before-and-after wording.
    Why it's better: Google says AI features are still grounded in standard SEO and snippet eligibility, so concise answer blocks matter more than gimmicks.
    OP-06

    Plan Internal Links with Crawlable Anchor Text

    ▼
    For this page — [URL/topic] — propose an internal linking plan using only crawlable HTML link patterns. Suggest 10 internal link opportunities from related pages, the ideal anchor text for each, and the user reason for clicking each link. Avoid generic anchors like 'click here' and avoid keyword-stuffed anchors.
    Why it's better: Google explicitly says crawlable <a href> links and descriptive anchors help users and crawlers understand page relationships.
    OP-07

    Improve Homepage Search Appearance Signals

    ▼
    Audit my homepage search appearance signals using this input — homepage copy: [paste], site name: [preferred name], alternative brand forms: [list], logo asset notes: [notes]. Recommend improvements for homepage title, site-name consistency, favicon readiness, WebSite structured data, and Organization structured data so Google can better disambiguate the brand and display the preferred site name.
    Why it's better: Site names, logos, and organization details are now documented parts of modern search appearance work.
    TS-01

    Audit Robots.txt Correctly

    ▼
    Review this robots.txt file — [paste file] — and tell me whether it is being used correctly for crawl management. Flag rules that accidentally block important content, CSS, JavaScript, images, or mobile pages. Also identify any places where the site appears to be using robots.txt as a deindexing method and recommend safer alternatives such as noindex or access controls where appropriate.
    Why it's better: It matches Google's documented use of robots.txt for crawl management, not deindexing.
    TS-02

    Design a Realistic Sitemap Strategy

    ▼
    Design an XML sitemap strategy for a site with these sections: [list sections]. Recommend whether to use one sitemap or a sitemap index, which content types deserve dedicated sitemaps, and how to handle canonical URLs, redirected URLs, excluded URLs, and non-indexable pages. Also tell me what should be submitted in Search Console and what should only live in site generation logic.
    Why it's better: It treats sitemaps as structured crawl signals, not as magical indexing guarantees.
    TS-03

    Diagnose Canonicalization Problems

    ▼
    Using this set of URLs, canonicals, redirect rules, and duplicate templates — [paste] — identify canonicalization issues. Flag conflicting canonical signals, parameter duplicates, pagination edge cases, and pages that should use self-referential canonicals. Tell me when rel=canonical, when redirects, and when content consolidation is the correct fix.
    Why it's better: Google's documentation is explicit that canonicals need clear, aligned signals and should not be replaced with robots or removal tricks.
    TS-04

    Fix Faceted Navigation and Filter URL Bloat

    ▼
    Audit this ecommerce or directory URL pattern — [paste examples] — for crawl waste, duplicate intent, and unstable parameters. Recommend which filter combinations deserve indexable landing pages, which URLs should remain crawlable but non-indexable, which should consolidate to canonicals, and how the internal link paths should surface the most important pages.
    Why it's better: It's tailored to one of the most common technical SEO failure points on large sites.
    TS-05

    Check Mobile Parity for Indexing

    ▼
    Compare the desktop and mobile versions of this page or template — [paste rendered output or notes]. Identify differences in core content, headings, metadata, structured data, media, internal links, and robots directives. Tell me which differences are harmless and which can hurt mobile-first indexing.
    Why it's better: Google still indexes based on the mobile version, so parity prompts are essential.
    TS-06

    Audit JavaScript Rendering Risks

    ▼
    Review this JavaScript-heavy page setup — [paste framework notes, rendered HTML, and raw HTML if available] — and identify what content or links might be invisible before rendering, what canonical or robots risks exist, and what should be server-rendered, hydrated, or exposed in raw HTML. Include a testing checklist using rendered HTML inspection.
    Why it's better: It tracks Google's documented crawl-render-index model for JavaScript.
    TS-07

    Strengthen Crawlable Site Architecture

    ▼
    Map the ideal crawl path for this site structure — [paste sitemap, nav, or URL tree]. Show how homepage, category, subcategory, and detail pages should link to each other using crawlable HTML links. Identify orphan pages, over-deep pages, thin hub pages, and pages that should receive stronger internal-link prominence.
    Why it's better: Google says it infers relative importance from links more than from URL shape alone.
    TS-08

    Prioritize Core Web Vitals and Page Experience

    ▼
    Given these performance diagnostics — [paste PageSpeed / CrUX / Search Console CWV issues] — create a prioritized remediation plan. Split fixes into LCP, INP, CLS, intrusive interstitials, and media/bundle issues. For each fix, tell me the likely user benefit, engineering complexity, and what should be tested first.
    Why it's better: It aligns the prompt with today's page-experience metrics, including INP.
    TS-09

    Audit Internationalization and Hreflang Setup

    ▼
    Review this multilingual site plan — languages/locales: [list], URL structure: [pattern], current alternate annotations: [paste if available]. Recommend the cleanest hreflang implementation method for this site — HTML, HTTP headers, or sitemap — and explain what each page set must contain, including self-references, full alternate sets, and fallbacks where useful.
    Why it's better: Google says the three implementation methods are equivalent, so the best choice is the one you can manage accurately.
    TS-10

    Add Bing and IndexNow Freshness Planning

    ▼
    Create a freshness and discovery workflow for Google and Bing using XML sitemaps, internal links, and IndexNow. The site type is [news/ecommerce/classifieds/docs/etc.] and the update frequency is [describe]. Tell me which URL changes should trigger immediate notifications, which should rely on sitemap updates, and how to avoid noisy or low-value submissions.
    Why it's better: It reflects Bing's 2026 guidance that SEO fundamentals also power Copilot and grounding, with IndexNow as an important freshness layer.
    CW-01

    Build a Non-Commodity Content Brief

    ▼
    Create a content brief for [topic] that explicitly avoids commodity content. I want: target audience, primary questions, unique angle, required first-hand evidence, expert inputs to collect, visual assets to create, competitor angles to avoid copying, and sections where original examples or data would make the page stronger.
    Why it's better: It pushes the model toward uniqueness and first-hand value instead of regurgitation.
    CW-02

    Extract First-Hand Experience Before Drafting

    ▼
    I am the subject-matter expert for [topic]. Interview me before writing. Ask me 20 questions designed to surface first-hand experience, mistakes made, outcomes observed, tools used, comparisons, edge cases, and advice I would give differently today. Then convert my answers into raw material for a high-trust article.
    Why it's better: Google's own guidance rewards experience and non-commodity insight more than polished generic copy.
    CW-03

    Create a Trust-Forward Article Outline

    ▼
    Using this topic — [topic] — create an article outline that makes trust visible. Include where to show the author, where to add methodology, where to cite evidence, where to explain limitations, where to add a last-updated note only if genuinely updated, and where to include practical next-step guidance for readers.
    Why it's better: It aligns content structure with trust, transparency, and useful page experience.
    CW-04

    Draft Author and Profile-Page Assets

    ▼
    For this author — [bio/details] — create: a concise byline, a fuller author bio, a profile page outline, and a structured-data-ready summary of credentials, experience, focus topics, and public profiles. Also tell me where this author information should appear across articles to strengthen trust and author disambiguation.
    Why it's better: Google documents both author clarity and profile-page markup as useful signals for understanding creators.
    CW-05

    Refresh Content Without Fake Freshness

    ▼
    Audit this existing article — [paste text or URL notes] — for refresh opportunities. Separate updates into: factual changes required, sections to expand, sections to remove, examples to replace, trust signals to add, and elements that should not be changed just to look 'fresh.' Recommend whether the page deserves a new dateModified signal or whether that would be misleading.
    Why it's better: Google warns against changing dates without substantial updates and puts the focus on meaningful improvement.
    CW-06

    Run an AI-Assisted Quality-Control Pass

    ▼
    Review this draft for AI-era content risks — [paste draft]. Flag sentences that sound generic, overconfident, repetitive, derivative, or unsupported. Then rewrite only the weak sections to sound more specific, more human, and more useful. Keep all claims faithful to the source material and do not invent evidence.
    Why it's better: It treats AI as a drafting helper, not a mass-content machine, which matches Google's guidance.
    CW-07

    Add Entity Markup That Matches Visible Content

    ▼
    Using this homepage or article page content — [paste] — recommend the most appropriate structured data from this list: Article, BlogPosting, Organization, WebSite, ProfilePage, Product, Breadcrumb, or none. Only include markup that is supported by the visible page content. Also tell me what should stay out of schema because it is not actually present on the page.
    Why it's better: Google explicitly says structured data should match visible content and be validated accurately.
    CW-08

    Apply a YMYL Trust Audit When Needed

    ▼
    This page is in a potentially YMYL area: [health/finance/legal/safety/etc.]. Audit the draft for trust risks, weak expertise signals, unsupported advice, and missing accountability elements. Then recommend what needs expert review, what disclaimers are useful, what evidence should be added, and what claims should be softened or removed.
    Why it's better: Google says strong E-E-A-T matters even more for topics that can affect health, finances, safety, or well-being.
    MX-01

    Triage Growth Opportunities from Search Console Insights

    ▼
    Use these Search Console Insights outputs — [paste] — to create an action plan. Separate pages and queries into: keep winning, refresh soon, urgent decline, and content expansion opportunities. Then tell me which opportunities are likely SEO fixes versus content fixes versus brand-demand effects.
    Why it's better: It converts simplified Insights data into practical editorial decisions.
    MX-02

    Combine Search Console and GA4 for Prioritization

    ▼
    Using this Search Console export and GA4 landing-page export — [paste] — rank my SEO opportunities by combining visibility, click-through rate, engagement, and conversions. Return a table with: URL, main query set, visibility score, engagement quality, conversion value, recommended action, and why this opportunity should or should not be prioritized.
    Why it's better: It mirrors Google's recommendation to use both tools together for a fuller picture.
    MX-03

    Audit AI-Feature Readiness Without Hype

    ▼
    Evaluate whether this page is well-positioned to be cited or surfaced in AI search experiences. Audit: indexability, snippet eligibility, internal discoverability, clarity of answer passages, usefulness of images/video, structured-data accuracy, and whether the content adds a unique perspective rather than repeating commodity facts. Do not recommend any fake 'GEO' hacks or special AI markup that Google does not require.
    Why it's better: Google explicitly says no special AI markup is needed and that standard SEO fundamentals still matter.
    MX-04

    Diagnose Losses After a Ranking Update

    ▼
    I saw a traffic drop around [date]. Create a post-update diagnosis workflow using Search Console. Tell me how to compare the right date ranges, how to separate small drops from large drops, how to isolate affected pages and queries, and how to distinguish between pages that need deeper improvements versus pages that should not be changed reactively.
    Why it's better: It follows Google's documented core-update analysis process and avoids panic edits.
    MX-05

    Build a Content Calendar from Demand Signals

    ▼
    Using this combination of Google Trends notes, Search Console queries, and business seasonality — [paste] — build a 6- or 12-month content calendar. Mark each topic as evergreen, seasonal, rising, declining, or event-driven. For each month, recommend net-new pages, refreshes, internal-link pushes, and promotional timing.
    Why it's better: It turns keyword insight into publishing operations.
    MX-06

    Spot CTR Rewrite Opportunities at Scale

    ▼
    From this Search Console page/query dataset — [paste] — identify pages with strong impressions but weak CTR relative to their average position. For each, hypothesize whether the problem is title clarity, snippet weakness, mismatched intent, weak branding, poor date signaling, or absent SERP-enhancing elements like better media or clearer page focus. Recommend the highest-confidence rewrite test.
    Why it's better: It uses real search behavior rather than theoretical copy preferences.
    MX-07

    Create a Publisher Preferred-Sources Plan

    ▼
    For this news or publishing site — [site details] — create a practical plan to grow direct audience preference and encourage readers to add the site as a preferred source. Include homepage messaging, on-site CTA placement, social promotion ideas, and audience segments most likely to respond. Also explain which parts of the strategy are brand growth and which are search-visibility support.
    Why it's better: Preferred sources is now part of Google's documented publisher surface, including AI features.
    MX-08

    Decide What to Measure After Publication

    ▼
    For this new page or campaign — [details] — create a 30-, 60-, and 90-day SEO measurement plan. Include which metrics belong in Search Console, which belong in Analytics, which patterns indicate simple maturation versus a real problem, and what threshold should trigger a title rewrite, content refresh, internal-link push, or technical audit.
    Why it's better: It keeps the workflow grounded in the tools Google itself recommends for monitoring search and on-site performance.
